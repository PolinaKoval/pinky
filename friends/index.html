pinky.

For friends

Help us test Pinky.

Pinky is a pinky promise to put your phone down: you and a friend agree how many minutes of Instagram a day are fine, and if one of you goes over, the other finds out. Before it goes public we need friends to use it for two weeks. Five minutes to set up, free, and you test in pairs: a promise needs two people.

Google opens an app to everyone only after 12 people have tested it for 14 days in a row, so every Android tester counts. iPhone testers don't count for Google, but they test the iPhone app, and a promise can mix Android and iPhone.

Android

  1. 1
    Join the testers group, signed in with the Google account your Play Store uses. If you were invited, the button says Accept invite; the "no permission" note under it is normal. Join the group
  2. 2
    Tap Become a tester: Google counts you only after this tap. Then Download it on Google Play. Android test onGoogle Play
  3. 3
    Open Pinky and switch on what it asks for: it shows each step. On Samsung, Xiaomi, Huawei, Oppo and similar phones, do the battery step too, or the phone puts Pinky to sleep at night.
  4. 4
    Keep Pinky installed and the test joined for 14 days. Leaving early resets Google's count.

"App not available"? Wait 15 minutes after joining the group. "Item not found"? Your Play Store is on another Google account: switch it in the Play Store. Had an old Pinky APK from us? Uninstall it first; Play can't install over it.

iPhone

  1. 1
    Install TestFlight from the App Store: Apple's app for trying apps before they're out. Pinky needs iOS 16 or newer.
  2. 2
    Open the TestFlight link on your iPhone and tap Accept, then Install. iPhone test onTestFlight
  3. 3
    Open Pinky, allow Screen Time when asked, pick Instagram and allow notifications. Pinky shows each step.
  4. 4
    Keep it for two weeks. TestFlight tells you when there is a new build; tap Update.

Then make the promise

One of you taps Make a promise, enters a name and agrees the deal: minutes a day and a sleep time. Tap Share invite and send it. The other taps the link and joins. Pinky Plus, the game, is sold through the stores and would cost you real money: ask us first, and we set you up so the test purchase is free.

What to try

Use your phone as normal. If you feel like it, tick these off in any order.

First days

  • Open Instagram for longer than your daily minutes. Your friend should hear "Caught!" within seconds.
  • Open it for less than a minute. Nobody should hear about it.
  • Open it in sleep time. You should see the pink stop screen, or a notification, first.
  • Send a nudge from the notification, and write a message from Home.
  • Miss a notification on purpose. The story on Home should still have it.

During the two weeks

  • Leave the phone untouched overnight. Is Pinky still watching the next morning?
  • Restart the phone once. Does Pinky come back by itself?
  • Change the deal: tap Edit next to OUR PROMISE, turn a wheel, tap Ask … to agree. Your friend gets a popup and taps Agree; both phones should show the new deal.
  • Add a watched app, then drop one. Dropping needs your friend's yes.
  • With Plus: set a stake and a streak goal, and read the morning report.

Tell us what happened

Anything that surprised you, broke or annoyed you. Short is fine, and "all fine" is useful too: please write once around day 7 and once at the end.

  • Email reunite.studio.app@gmail.com with what you did, what you expected and what happened. A screenshot helps.
  • "It didn't notify"? In Pinky open Settings, then Health, tap Copy and paste it into the email. It shows whether Pinky was watching and connected, and nothing private.
  • iPhone: take a screenshot and share it from TestFlight (Send Beta Feedback). It attaches the details for you.
  • Android: Pinky's page in the Play Store takes private feedback that only we see.

What Pinky sees

Which of the apps you chose is on screen, and for how long. Never what you do inside them, never your messages, and nothing from a phone that isn't in your promise. Your friend gets minute totals, not a play-by-play. The privacy policy has the full list.
